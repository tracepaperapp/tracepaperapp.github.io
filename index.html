Composable architecture

We designed a composable architecture that builds on the strengths of AWS Serverless — providing the foundation for every Tracepaper-powered application.

Tracepaper is designed for ownership

You model locally — on your own machine — using the Tracepaper Modeler.

When you trigger a build, the Tracepaper Build Agent in the cloud generates clean, production-ready code — deployment-safe by design.

That code lives in your own repository, fully under your control. From there, your AWS pipeline takes over, deploying your infrastructure through CloudFormation.

Tracepaper doesn’t host or run your app — it simply gives you the power to own it.

In plain English

Tracepaper lets you design software the way an architect designs a building — by working with structure, intent, and behavior instead of raw code.

You don’t start by programming. You describe what should happen: what actions people can take, what events follow, and how the system should respond.

Once your model is ready, Tracepaper turns it into real, working software — complete with databases, logic, and cloud deployment scripts, ready to run in your own AWS account.

So instead of wrestling with infrastructure, you focus on clarity — designing how your system thinks and reacts — while Tracepaper takes care of the technical scaffolding.

You stay in control of the design. The computer just does the heavy lifting.

The Tracepaper Modeler

The Tracepaper Modeler is built like an IDE — familiar, structured, and intentionally simple.

On the left, your domain files; in the center, your active model; on the right, contextual views that help you navigate, reason, and refine.

Everything is connected — change the model, and the diagram updates instantly.

It’s not drag-and-drop; it’s domain-driven design, made visual.

Scenarios as quality gates

This clip gives a short overview of Scenarios in Tracepaper.

A scenario is essentially a sequence of steps executed against the GraphQL API of your deployed application — a mix of mutations and queries that simulate real interactions.

You can define expectations for each step: when mutation X is triggered, behavior Y should succeed — or perhaps behavior Z should fail. Queries can then validate whether the API state matches what you expect, or extract data to feed into subsequent mutations.

Scenarios can run independently or in relation to each other: for instance, A and B may run in parallel, but both must complete before C begins.

Scenarios always run against a deployed environment — typically staging or a feature branch. For the main branch, they act as a quality gate: only when all scenarios pass, the build is promoted from staging to production.

Feedback built into the modeling process

Tracepaper is designed around short feedback loops — each operating on a different level of depth and timescale.

At the inner loop, model validation happens instantly. The modeler continuously checks structure, syntax, and dependencies while you work, ensuring your model remains consistent in real time.

The middle loop consists of behavior specifications, which run automatically with every build. They verify within seconds that your domain logic behaves exactly as defined in your functional model.

The outer loop, scenarios, runs in minutes and validates the system end-to-end — across aggregates, APIs, and event flows. It confirms that everything still holds true once your model is deployed.

These nested loops work together to give you constant assurance: instant correctness while modeling, behavioral integrity at build time, and full confidence at runtime.

Everything at your fingertips

From the Settings page, you have direct access to everything that connects your model to the real world.

You can open your local project folder — where both your model and generated code live — or jump straight into your AWS pipeline, the quality gate report in DynamoDB, and even the AppSync console for your API — all just one click away.

When you set up your deployment, Tracepaper opens an external link to AWS CloudFormation. It loads a preconfigured template hosted by us and passes your project parameters automatically. You complete the setup in AWS — guided step by step by Tracepaper’s built-in wizard and documentation.

So while your deployment happens in your own AWS account, Tracepaper ensures the entire process feels seamless and fully under your control.

Navigating your model

Modeling in Tracepaper isn’t just about creating structure — it’s about understanding how everything fits together.

As your domain grows, so does its complexity. That’s why Tracepaper offers multiple ways to navigate your model — each offering a different lens on the same system.

The File Browser lets you move through the model’s hierarchy — it’s your structural map. The Tree Map helps you see the scale of your system — how subdomains, aggregates, and features compare in size and scope. The Context Diagram shows how information flows through your model — from commands to events and views. And Search lets you instantly jump to any concept, no matter where it lives.

Together, these perspectives let you move seamlessly between the fine detail and the bigger picture — between structure, behavior, and meaning.

Navigating through structure

The File Browser gives you a structural view of your model — it mirrors the folder layout of your domain, so you can immediately see how everything fits together.

When you click a model title — the colored block in the top right — the browser automatically centers on that element: all folders along its path open up, while unrelated branches collapse.

It’s a simple yet powerful way to stay oriented — always showing exactly where you are within the hierarchy of your domain.

Understanding the shape of your domain

The Tree Map gives you a visual overview of your entire domain. Each block represents part of your model — subdomains, aggregates, commands, and views — scaled by their relative size.

It helps you spot imbalances at a glance: which aggregates are growing large, where behavior concentrates, or which areas of your model might need refactoring.

It’s not about relationships — it’s about proportion. The Tree Map helps you see your architecture as a living ecosystem.

Seeing behavior in context

The Context Diagram shows how each part of your model interacts — commands triggering events, events updating views, and notifiers connecting the dots.

It’s more than a visualization. It’s a living map of cause and effect — helping you reason about flow, ownership, and impact without leaving the model.

Many modelers find this their favorite view — it’s where logic becomes visible, and architecture starts to tell its own story.

Finding your way back

The Search view was the last navigation feature we added — born from real modeling sessions where I simply lost track of where things lived.

It’s a quick way to jump straight to any element in your model. Type a few letters, find your entry point, and continue exploring — usually through the Context Diagram right after.

It’s simple, fast, and surprisingly powerful — a small feature that makes large models feel manageable again.

Modeling with confidence

Every change you make in Tracepaper is tracked — whether it touches one file or several parts of your model at once.

The Change List groups related edits together, making undo and redo feel natural, even across complex updates.

You can explore your recent history, see what was added, removed, or modified, and revert entire modeling steps in one go.

Of course, your project still lives inside your own Git repository — so good commit hygiene remains essential. Tracepaper does its best to keep you safe, but your stack is the ultimate source of truth.

Documentation built in

Tracepaper comes with embedded documentation — always available, even when you’re offline.

Wherever you see the lightbulb icon, you can open the docs directly in context, showing only the information relevant to what you’re modeling.

The documentation mirrors how Tracepaper itself is structured — from commands and events to projections and notifiers — so it feels like a natural extension of the model.

Beyond modeling concepts, you’ll also find practical guides on the generated code and deployment setup: things we can’t automate, like linking your AWS or GitHub account.

It’s all designed to keep you moving forward — with help that’s right where you need it.

Pricing

Tracepaper’s pricing reflects how you design, not how much you consume.

You run your applications in your own AWS account — your cloud costs scale with traffic. Tracepaper scales with architectural capability: the set of modeling features that shape how your system behaves.

The model is simple: Seats cover your use of the modeler — the cognitive layer. The base fee licenses your generated architecture for commercial use — the runtime layer. Once licensed, all core modeling concepts are unlimited. You only pay extra when you extend your model with advanced capabilities such as additional Notifiers, Projections, or Expressions.

In short: you don’t pay for traffic or runtime load — you pay for the features that make your architecture smarter and your work simpler.
